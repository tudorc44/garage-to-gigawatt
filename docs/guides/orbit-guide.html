How Orbit works

A player's guide to the Orbit board: putting compute in space as blocks, getting them launched on scarce rockets, signing tenants, paying for it all, and keeping one bad launch from sinking the company.

Prices quoted are the 2031 market's. They move from 2032 depending on the future you're in. No spoilers: what the game hides stays hidden here too.

1 · Orbit at a glance

Orbit sits beside your ground business on the same balance sheet. You open it from Orbit in the left menu. Every orbital investment is a block: a batch of satellites carrying compute, powered by their own solar panels. A block is a project card with three slots, just like an Act II ground project.

  • 1 · Plan phaseOpen a blockPick kind, size, shell, satellites.
  • 2 · Plan phaseFill three slotsLaunch, Tenant, Capital, plus a licence with room.
  • 3 · 2 quartersBuildingStarts by itself when everything's ready.
  • 4 · launch quarterLaunchAt the quarter's end: it flies, slips or fails.
  • 5 · 1 quarterClimbingRising to its orbit.
  • 6 · yearsLiveEarns every quarter until it deorbits or you sell.

All orbit decisions happen in the Plan phase. During the live quarter, orbit can only interrupt you with a conjunction alert or a solar storm warning. Launches, slips and failures are settled at the quarter's end and reported in the log and the quarter report.

2 · Your first block, step by step

  1. File a licence first. In Licences and registry, press File on Dawn-dusk SSO (1 BW + $1.0M). It covers 200 MW and is approved 2 quarters later. A block can't start building without licence room, so file early.
  2. Decide your flag (under "Whose flag your satellites fly") before filing: changing it is free until your first filing and costs 1 BW after. The Accords-bloc flag is the default and unlocks export credit.
  3. Open a block. The defaults are fine to start: orbital shell, 10 MW, Dawn-dusk SSO, newest satellites. 0 BW
  4. Sign a tenant. Two offers arrive with the block; Sign one (0 BW), or Sell on spot instead.
  5. Book a launch 2 to 6 quarters ahead (1 BW, 15% deposit). Booking 2 quarters ahead lines up with the 2-quarter build.
  6. Arrange the capital: pick Own cash, Export-credit loan, Project debt or Sovereign co-funding, then Arrange (1 BW).
  7. Insure the launch with Insure… (0 BW) and check the Launch exposure panel shows no warning for the block.
  8. End the Plan phase. If the licence is approved, all slots are filled and you have the cash, the build starts.

3 · Blocks

Two kinds

KindWhat you buildHow it earns
Orbital shellThe satellites and their power. The tenant brings the computers.Rent per MW a year ($6.0M/MW-yr in 2031).
Orbital cloudThe satellites and the GPUs (600 per MW, with 20% spares).GPU-hours ($3.60/GPU-hr in 2031), much more per MW but far dearer to build.

Sizes, satellites and mass

  • Sizes: 5, 10, 25, 50 or 100 MW.
  • Satellites: Gen 31 (18 t per MW) from 2031. Newer generations are lighter, from 2033 (Gen 33) and, in some futures, from late 2034 (Gen 35). The form only lists a generation once it exists.
  • Mass = tonnes per MW × MW, plus shielding for higher shells (+10% High LEO, +30% High orbit). Mass decides launch cost and whether the block fits on a launch at all.
  • Build: 2 quarters, paid in full when it starts. Running costs are $0.25M per MW a year.
  • Design life: 5 years. The block deorbits at the end of its real life, which you don't see.

4 · The three slots

Launch

Book one provider for one quarter, 2 to 6 quarters ahead, at a price per kg locked when you book.

Book 1 BW · 15% deposit now, the rest on launch day.

Tenant

Two offers arrive with the block, and fresh ones each quarter while the slot is empty. Offers are signed as drawn: no haggling.

Sign 0 BW · or Sell on spot instead.

Capital

Choose how the build and launch are paid for. Set it while the block is in Planning.

Arrange 1 BW, even for own cash.

On top of the slots, the block needs licence room in its shell and the cash for your share. The build starts by itself at the end of the Plan phase once everything is in place. If it can't, the log says why ("no licence room in its shell yet" or "it needs … in cash").

Rent and GPU-hour prices are the same in every shell. You pay more to go higher and get calm in return.

  • Debris. Each quarter a live block can be struck, losing a quarter of its capacity. The chance grows steeply with congestion: noticeable in the busy shell, close to nothing higher up.
  • The busy shell can close. If Dawn-dusk SSO ever closes, new launches into it slip until it reopens, and the blocks already there can be badly damaged. Whether that happens is not something the game tells you in advance.
  • Each shell needs its own licence.

6 · Licences and your flag

  • File 1 BW + $1.0M licenses 200 MW in one shell. Filing again in the same shell adds 200 MW more.
  • Approval takes 2 quarters (3 under the neutral flag). The panel always shows the quarter it would be approved.
  • Fast-track costs 10 political capital (0 BW) and takes a quarter off a pending filing, once per licence.
  • A block uses licence MW from the day its build starts until it leaves orbit. A failed launch frees it.
The use-it-or-lose-it milestone. At the end of 2035 Q2, in each shell, at least 25% of your licensed MW must be live. If not, every licence in that shell is cut in half. Don't stockpile licences you won't fill.

7 · Launches

The launch manifest

Launch capacity is scarce. The Launch manifest lists the next 7 quarters: third-party slots (in tonnes), what you've booked, and your launches. In 2031 there are 400 t of slots a quarter. Quarters less than 2 ahead are "too soon to book".

  • One block, one launch. A block can't be split across rockets, so its whole mass must fit in that quarter's free slots. A 25 MW Gen 31 block in SSO weighs 450 t and won't fit a 400 t quarter.
  • Book no later than 2035 Q4.

Providers

Launch day (end of the launch quarter)

  • It slips one quarter if the provider slips, the launcher is grounded, the shell is closed, or Carrack bumps you. After any slip, your deposit becomes refundable.
  • It flies: you pay the remaining 85%. It climbs next quarter and is live the quarter after.
  • It fails: the build, the deposit and the launch are lost. Insurance pays up to its cover, and repays the block's lender first. The block goes back to Planning with its tenant and capital choice kept. Rebook it (1 BW) and the satellites are rebuilt at that quarter's prices.

  • A tenant expects the block live by its due quarter: the booked launch quarter + 2. Every quarter late costs 3% of a year's contract value as a penalty. Slips and failures don't move the due date.
  • When the term ends, the block sells on spot.
  • Spot instead of a tenant: shells earn 80% of market rent; clouds sell GPU-hours at market price with only 70% of them used (a tenant pays for 85%). No tenant also means no project debt.

10 · Running a live block

  • Income is paid at the quarter's end: a quarter of the yearly value × the share of capacity still working (minus a co-funder's share). The card shows last quarter's EBITDA.
  • Fleet telemetry shows noisy readings of how often GPUs fail, against the 8% a year everyone planned on. Small blocks give noisier readings. A cloud starts with 20% spare GPUs; once the spares run out, failures eat capacity ("GPUs working …, spares left …").

Links to the ground

Only interactive work (Kite Inference) needs links: 1 link unit per 5 MW. Without enough, the block earns only the share its links carry.

  • Rent units with − / + (0 BW): $0.8M per unit a year.
  • Build an optical ground station at one of your sites (1 BW, $15M): 4 units from next quarter, but +3 Heat at that site. One per site.

Alerts during the live quarter

11 · Risk and insurance

Insurance

  • Before launch: Insure <cover> for <premium> (<rate>) 0 BW. Cover is the block's value, up to the market's capacity per launch ($350M in 2031). It lasts through the launch and the first year live.
  • The rate tells you about the rocket: 3% for a mature launcher, 7.5% for a young one (2031).
  • In orbit: a yearly renewal at 2% (2031).
  • Hard market: one loss of $400M or more makes insurance 75% dearer and 30% scarcer for a year ("The insurance market is hard until …").

The Launch exposure panel

Shown on the Plan screen and the Orbit board, it warns about any block heading to launch where:

  • the uninsured value riding on it is more than 15% of your equity;
  • your cash would go negative after paying its launch bill; or
  • losing it would push your leverage past the covenant limit.
The safety promise. If you keep each launch inside those three lines, no single launch failure forces a sale or breaks a covenant. Insure to the market's capacity, spread big plans over several blocks and quarters, and keep cash for the bills.

12 · Selling, fire sales, auctions

  • Sell a live block: Sell for … 1 BW. Price = last quarter's EBITDA × 4 × the space multiple × 0.8. The lender is repaid first.
  • Fire sale: if you're short of cash at a quarter's end, the game first sells your smallest Act IV asset that covers the gap, at about half a normal sale price (EBITDA × 4 × multiple × 0.4), before raising emergency equity.
  • Orrery Compute's auction: if this rival ever fails, its live blocks go up for sale for 2 quarters: 50 MW in the busy shell at $6M per MW ($300M), with about 3 years of life left. Buy them 1 BW. They arrive uninsured and selling on spot.

13 · How orbit counts in your valuation

  • Live blocks are valued at orbital EBITDA × the space multiple (14× in 2031). The multiple moves with the market, so the same fleet can be worth much more or much less over time.
  • Blocks building, waiting or climbing count at what you've spent on them.
  • The quarter report's Orbit and Moon this quarter panel shows blocks live and under way, revenue, EBITDA and the value at the multiple. The League compares you with the space rivals.

15 · What you can't see

Read the Signals, not the future. These are hidden until the chapter report at the end of 2035:
  • Which of the four futures you're in, and what moves it. From 2032, launch prices, slots, rents, GPU-hour prices, the space multiple and congestion all follow it.
  • How often orbital GPUs really fail, and how long blocks really last. You only get telemetry and the 5-year design life.
  • How likely each rocket is to fail. You can infer young versus mature from the insurance rate.
  • Which wildcards are in your game (2 of 6), and when they land: a grounded launcher, a chip export clampdown, a solar storm, and others.

The Signals panel adds six Act IV indicators: Launch Quotes, Fleet Reliability, Orbital Congestion, Ground Power Squeeze, Compute Demand Gap and Regulatory Climate. Like Act III's, they're noisy public hints, and reading one closely costs Bandwidth.

16 · Traps worth knowing

  • Late penalties keep running after a failed launch. The tenant's due date doesn't move, so a rebuild costs 3% of a year's contract value every quarter you're late.
  • A launch booked before the build can finish moves to when the satellites are ready, which can make you late too.
  • Big blocks may not fit. In 2031 only 400 t of slots exist a quarter, so 25 MW and up in Gen 31 can't fly yet. Kestrel takes at most 250 t.
  • Kite Inference needs links. Rent units or build a ground station, or the block earns only part of its revenue.
  • Project debt watches your insurance. Let cover drop below half the loan and the lender calls it after 2 quarters.
  • The licence milestone halves licences that are less than a quarter live by the end of 2035 Q2.
  • A block's sale price shows $0 in its first live quarter, until its first quarter of earnings is booked.
