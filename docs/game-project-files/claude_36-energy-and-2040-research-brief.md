# 36: Research brief: power options across the eras, energy ventures, and the world to 2040 (design thread, 9 Oct 2026)

**What this is:** the brief for one research dossier (to become doc 37). It grounds three planned features:
1. cheaper power options in the early eras (Prologue → Act II), including batteries;
2. energy ventures (Acts III–IV, delivering into Act V): backing new firm power, with pumped storage;
3. a possible Act V (2036–2040).

Nothing here is decided. The dossier informs the design passes that follow.

**Who runs it:** a research session with web access (as doc 31 was produced). It is not the build thread.

## Rules (same as doc 31)
- **Grade every claim:**
  - [A] primary or official source;
  - [B] reputable reporting or company statement;
  - [C] analyst, forecast or industry estimate;
  - [D] speculative, or inference with no source.
- Mark **inference** separately from sourced facts.
- **Give ranges with their basis** when sources disagree, and say which you prefer and why.
- **Date everything** (the publication date and the date accessed).
- **Never invent a figure.** If a number the game needs can't be found, say so and give the closest proxy, labelled as such.
- **Real company names are reference points only.** The game will use fictional ventures modelled on real archetypes (the D15 lesson from Act III). Report what real companies announced and achieved, but don't predict their outcomes.
- **For every item, end with "What the game needs"** in the same units: capex ($/kW or $/kWh), running cost ($/MWh), capacity factor or hours covered, build time (quarters), lifetime, slip, overrun and failure base rates, region or site constraints, and side effects (Heat, permits, grid). Convert to these units yourself and show the working.

## Part A: Power options, Prologue to Act II (2009–2026)
1. **Residential rooftop solar, 2009–2022:**
   - installed $/W by year (US, and one European market);
   - typical capacity factor;
   - what share of a 24/7 load (a 0.5–2 kW home mining rig) it covers.
2. **Small wind for homes and small sites:** installed cost, real-world capacity factors, and why small wind underperforms. The game may use it as a deliberate trap; confirm whether that's fair.
3. **Batteries:**
   - lithium-ion pack and installed system $/kWh by year, 2010–2026, residential and utility;
   - credible projections to 2040;
   - round-trip efficiency, cycle life and degradation;
   - what solar + storage needs to cover a 24/7 load (oversizing ratio, hours of storage), with a worked example for a home rig in 2015 and a 1 MW site in 2022.
4. **"Utilities included" mining:** documented cases of early miners using flat-rate or included power (student housing, rentals), and how they ended (landlord or utility reactions). Anecdotes are fine if graded.
5. **Hydro mining regions, 2013–2022:**
   - Washington's mid-Columbia PUDs (Chelan, Douglas, Grant), upstate New York (Plattsburgh, Massena) and Quebec (Hydro-Québec);
   - power rates, how miners got allocations, and the backlash timeline (moratoria, special rates, application floods).
6. **Iceland:** power prices and contract terms for miners and data centres 2014–2022; the cooling benefit; setup and shipping costs; why the boom slowed (power shortages, policy).
7. **Flare and stranded gas mining (2018+):** the economics ($/MWh effective), container deployment, well decline, relocation, regulation, and the main operators' history.
8. **On-site solar and wind for miners and AI sites (Act I–II):** capex per MW by year, capacity factors by region (Texas, Georgia, the Nordics…), behind-the-meter rules.
9. **Texas:**
   - wind and solar power purchase agreements;
   - the share of negative-price hours in ERCOT by year;
   - how miners used them (demand response, curtailment credits).

## Part B: Storage at scale
1. **Utility batteries 2020–2040:** $/kWh, $/kW, duration (2–8 hours), what they earn (buying power cheap and selling at peak, capacity payments, ancillary services), and capacity-market treatment (PJM, ERCOT).
2. **Pumped hydro:**
   - share of global grid storage (historically and now);
   - capex per kW and per kWh;
   - build times;
   - siting needs (two reservoirs, elevation, water);
   - overrun and delay examples (e.g. Snowy 2.0) and base rates;
   - closed-loop and former-mine projects;
   - who owns them and how a third party takes a stake.
3. **Long-duration storage (multi-day, e.g. iron-air):** technology status, first deployments, cost claims versus outcomes.

## Part C: Energy ventures, firm power 2026–2040
For each technology:
- the planned milestones of the leading developers;
- their historical slip and overrun record;
- expected $/MWh at first-of-a-kind and at scale;
- what offtake and pre-purchase deals look like (structure, prepayment, price terms);
- base rates for first-of-a-kind energy projects (delay, overrun, cancellation).

1. **Enhanced geothermal** (e.g. Fervo's Cape Station and its Google deal; doc 31 notes a 2026 start, up to 500 MW).
2. **Small modular and advanced fission** (e.g. Kairos–Google by 2030, TerraPower Natrium–Meta by 2032, X-energy, NuScale's 2023 cancellation and its Romania plan).
3. **Fusion** (e.g. Helion–Microsoft 2028, Commonwealth Fusion's ARC early 2030s): what it would take for fusion power on the grid by 2040, versus the "not before 2035" assumption in doc 33.
4. **Others worth a line, if credible:** gas with carbon capture, hydrogen, space solar (Act IV already covers orbit).

Close Part C with a table: technology × earliest credible first power × likely first power × chance of commercial power by 2035 and by 2040 (graded, with basis).

## Part D: The world 2036–2040 (for a possible Act V)
1. **Data-centre and AI electricity demand to 2040:** IEA and others beyond 2035; the range across cases.
2. **AI compute and chips to 2040:** the published roadmap horizon, efficiency trends, the credible range.
3. **Orbit:** the cost-parity estimates (doc 31: SemiAnalysis ~2040), launch cost trajectories after 2035, congestion and regulation outlook.
4. **The Moon:** lunar resource and production timelines after 2035 (doc 33 lets a production plant be contracted for after 2035).
5. **Bitcoin:** the 2032, 2036 and 2040 halvings; any credible hashprice or security-budget analyses for the 2030s.
6. **Quantum:** published estimates for cryptographically relevant quantum computers ("Q-Day") and post-quantum migration timelines.
7. **Grid and policy to 2040:** interconnection reform, climate targets for 2035/2040, nuclear policy, and community opposition trends (doc 31 has 2026 data).
8. **Candidate scenarios for 2036–2040:** 4–6 distinct, published-case-based futures (as doc 31 did for Act IV), each with its leading indicators. Also: what a decoy might look like.
